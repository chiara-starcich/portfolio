Walking together, not just for money

A step challenge app redesigned around a community and a second currency, hearts.

TypeeHealth app + reward system
TeamWith Veronique Serné
ContextTU Delft
Year2025 to 26
The situation

An app that pays you to walk. Mostly men joined.

My role
  • Research & 2 questionnaires
  • Personas & user journeys
  • Hearts system, UI refinement
Result

Hearts reward helping others. Money still comes only from walking.

Chiara Starcich · Portfolio 2026HealthBlocks

Challenge

Built on competition and money.
Mostly men joined.

Motivation depends on context, not gender. So we designed for women aged 20 to 45, without stereotypes.

How might wegrow HealthBlocks and attract more women, while keeping people active?
Chiara Starcich · Portfolio 2026HealthBlocks

Insight

Money gets people started.
A community keeps them going.

Key user: the socially motivated

Money doesn’t motivate her. A supportive group does.

Chiara Starcich · Portfolio 2026HealthBlocks

Solution

Summits,
not leaderboards.

Home

Daily goal on top, friends and groups below.

Summit pools

One recommended pool, joined in one tap.

Community

Hearts, groups and what you achieved together.

Chiara Starcich · Portfolio 2026HealthBlocks

Outcome

From a list of pools to a summit you choose.

→

What reshaped it

Failed

Money and competition alone → Groups and summits

Constraint

Hearts can’t be cashed in → Money only from steps

Constraint

Paid challenges had to stay → Reframed as summits

What I learned

Designing for women doesn’t mean designing “for women”.

Lunch delivered to campus

One rider brings lunch from the canteen, bakeries and delis to campus at 1 pm, with discounts that cut food waste.

TypeService + app design
TeamTeam of 4
ContextUniversità di Ferrara
Year2023
The situation

One hour for lunch. The canteen is too far.

My role
  • Survey, interviews, field research
  • Storyboard & paper prototypes
  • Final UI design
Result

A tested service and app that also cuts food waste.

Chiara Starcich · Portfolio 2026Unifood

Challenge

One hour for lunch,
a canteen too far away.

The problem wasn’t the food: one hour isn’t enough to go and get it.

1

Lunch on campus within the break.

2

Yesterday’s leftovers at a student discount.

Chiara Starcich · Portfolio 2026Unifood

Process

Sketch, test, iterate.

1 · Paper sketches
2 · Mid fidelity in Figma
Tested with 4 students

Order history unclear → Two tabs: upcoming and history

“How do I get the discount?” → Explained up front

Icons didn’t look tappable → Clearer icons, fewer words

Chiara Starcich · Portfolio 2026Unifood

Solution

Plan the week.
Pick up at 1 pm.

Meal plan

Today’s discounts, straight to your plan.

Orders

Today’s order and your history.

Pickup points

The one nearest your lecture.

Chiara Starcich · Portfolio 2026Unifood

Outcome

How an order travels.

1

Pick a seller

Canteen, deli, bakery or fruit shop

2

Pay in the app

Cheaper in advance, or yesterday’s leftovers

3

Choose a pickup point

On campus, near your lecture

4

One rider delivers

Everything, at 1 pm

Result

Lunch within the break, cheaper for students, less waste for sellers.

What I learned

Sometimes the solution is a service, not a screen.

03

DOC

A calming companion for ER patients

A device that hangs on the bed rail and explains vitals through screen, sound and touch.

TypeProduct + screen
TeamTeam of 4
ContextErasmus MC
Length3 months
The situation

Patients wait alone next to a monitor they can’t read.

My role
  • Led test design & testing
  • Sound design
  • Screen design
Result

People felt watched over and calmer.

Chiara Starcich · Portfolio 2026DOC

Challenge

Two hours, one beeping machine,
nobody to ask.

The machine meant to protect them scares them.

How might wehelp ER patients feel safe and in control while they wait?
Chiara Starcich · Portfolio 2026DOC

Insight

Patients didn’t want more numbers.
They wanted to know they were okay.

Chiara Starcich · Portfolio 2026DOC

Process

Every test changed the design.

Card sort

No more numbers, just “am I okay?”

→

An avatar and simple meters

VR sound test

Beeps raised anxiety

→

Calm sounds; beeps only in emergencies

Touch test

Hours in bed made people restless

→

Textures to squeeze and hold

Final test

A kind tone calmed people

→

“You’re being looked after”

Chiara Starcich · Portfolio 2026DOC

Outcome

Calm on the rail. Calm in your hands.

Result

People felt watched over and calmer.

What I learned

Tone, sound and touch calmed people more than data.

How do doctors rely on AI?

A method and a clickable toolbox for Philips usability designers.

TypeDigital tool for experts
TeamSolo graduation project
ContextPhilips, UMC Utrecht, TU Delft
Year2026
The situation

AI is never 100% right. Designers had no method to study reliance.

My role
  • 7 expert interviews
  • Concept & method
  • Expert tests, UX/UI
Result

Philips plans to use it in a real usability study.

Chiara Starcich · Portfolio 2026Reliance Toolbox

Challenge

AI is never 100% right.
What matters is what doctors do with it.

The field lab: a real hospital ward to test new tech with real data, safely.

How might wehelp designers test, over time, how doctors rely on AI?
7 interviews: reliance changes with
FatigueAI errorsWorkloadFamiliarity

Designers needed guidance, not another tool.

Chiara Starcich · Portfolio 2026Reliance Toolbox

Process

Four structures. Experts picked the hub.

7

expert interviews

5

experts tested the concepts

2

rounds of expert testing

Chiara Starcich · Portfolio 2026Reliance Toolbox

Solution

Learn the basics, or jump to the tools.

Compare and filter

Every tool on 9 criteria, before, during and after the test.

A hub, not a path

Four sections, a way back from each.

A real example

A one page example study.

Chiara Starcich · Portfolio 2026Reliance Toolbox

Outcome

Freedom, with guidance and a real example.

Field lab visit

Doctors have very little time

→

A table to pick tools that fit the ward

Interviews

Reliance changes over time

→

Tools grouped before, during, after

Expert test 1

Experts wanted freedom

→

A hub instead of fixed steps

Expert test 2

Examples beat instructions

→

A one page example study

What I learned

Experts want freedom, with a good example. Listening first kept me from building the wrong method.
